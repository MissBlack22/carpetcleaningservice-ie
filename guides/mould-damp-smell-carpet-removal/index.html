Damp & Odour Guide

How to remove mould and damp smell from carpet

A musty or damp smell in carpet is a very common complaint in Dublin homes, especially older solid-floor houses. Here's why it happens, what you can reasonably do about it yourself, and — honestly — when cleaning alone won't be enough.

Why it happens so often in Dublin homes

A few things come together to make damp and musty carpet smells common here: a lot of Dublin's housing stock is older, with solid concrete floors that don't breathe the way a suspended timber floor does, so moisture has fewer places to go. Add in poor ventilation in a room, a damp sub-floor, condensation on cold external walls, or a spill that wasn't dried out properly at the time, and carpet — which holds onto moisture and odour more than a hard floor — ends up carrying the smell long after the original cause.

A damp smell usually means moisture is trapped underneath, not just on the surface

This is the key thing to understand before reaching for a carpet freshener. Carpet sits on top of underlay, which sits on the sub-floor. If moisture has got into that underlay or sub-floor — from a slow leak, rising damp, condensation, or a spill that soaked through — the smell you're noticing is often coming from underneath, not from the visible carpet fibres. Cleaning or deodorising the surface can mask the smell for a short while, but if the moisture source underneath isn't addressed, it tends to come back, particularly in humid weather or when the room isn't well ventilated.

Sensible first steps you can take yourself

Before assuming the worst, a few honest, low-cost steps are worth trying:

Improve ventilation

Open windows when weather allows, use extractor fans in kitchens and bathrooms, and avoid drying wet items on top of or near the affected carpet.

Use a dehumidifier

Running a dehumidifier in the affected room can reduce airborne moisture and slow down mould growth while you address the underlying cause.

Find and fix the source

Check for the actual cause — a leak, condensation on an external wall, a gap letting rain in, or rising damp — since no amount of carpet cleaning fixes a moisture problem that's still active.

If a spill or flood has happened, dry the area as thoroughly and quickly as you can — fans, ventilation, and lifting the edge of the carpet if it's safe to do so, to let air reach the underlay. The faster carpet dries out fully, the less chance mould or a lingering smell has to take hold in the first place.

When it's a job for a professional deep clean or inspection

It's worth calling in help when: you can see visible mould on or under the carpet, the musty smell persists after you've improved ventilation and used a dehumidifier for a week or two, the carpet has been damp for an extended period, or there's anyone in the household with asthma or allergies where damp and mould are more of a concern. A professional visit can assess how far moisture has actually spread through the carpet and underlay, and treat it properly rather than just refreshing the surface.

An honest note: sometimes cleaning isn't enough

We'd rather say this upfront than sell a clean that won't solve the problem. If underlay or sub-floor damp is severe, or carpet has been wet for a long time, replacement is sometimes the more realistic option rather than repeated cleaning. A good inspection should tell you honestly which situation you're in — and if it's a cleaning job, we're glad to help; if it genuinely isn't, we'll say so.
