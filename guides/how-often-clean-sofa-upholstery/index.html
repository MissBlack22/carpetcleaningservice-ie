How Often Should You Clean Your Sofa or Upholstery?

There's no single answer that fits every household — a sofa in a spare room and a sofa that two kids and a dog use every day are not on the same schedule. Here's what actually drives how often upholstery needs cleaning, what you can do yourself in between, and how to tell when it's time to book a professional clean.

What affects the schedule

The main factors that decide how often

How often a sofa or armchair needs cleaning comes down to how much it's used and what it's exposed to, more than any fixed calendar rule. A few things matter most:

Household size

More people using a sofa day to day means more general soiling — body oils, everyday dirt and wear build up faster with heavier use.

Pets

Hair, dander and the odd muddy paw print all add up. Households with pets on the furniture typically need cleaning more often than those without.

Kids

Spills, snacks and general activity mean upholstery in a family room usually needs more frequent attention than a formal sitting room.

Fabric type

Some fabrics show marks and hold onto dirt more readily than others. A tightly woven synthetic weave can look fresher for longer than a pale natural fibre in the same room.

Sunlight

Direct sun causes fading over time, which is a separate issue from soiling — cleaning won't reverse fading, but rotating cushions can even it out.

Where it sits

A sofa near a front door or in a main living space collects more everyday dirt than one in a rarely used room.

Everyday maintenance

What you can do yourself between cleans

Most of the work in keeping upholstery looking good happens between professional cleans, not instead of them. A few simple habits go a long way:

  • Vacuum regularly. A weekly pass with an upholstery attachment lifts dust and loose debris before it works its way deeper into the fabric.
  • Rotate and flip cushions. Turning seat cushions every so often spreads out wear and sun exposure evenly, rather than letting one spot take all of it.
  • Deal with spills straight away. Blotting a fresh spill immediately, rather than letting it sit, makes a real difference to whether it becomes a set-in stain.
  • Keep pets' claws trimmed and consider a throw on spots pets favour, if that's a regular issue.
  • Air the room when you can — good ventilation helps stop everyday odours building up in fabric.

When it's time for a professional clean

Signs a full clean is overdue

Vacuuming and quick spill response only go so far — they manage the surface, not what's worked its way into the fibres and fill underneath. A professional clean using hot water extraction reaches further, lifting out dirt and oils that day-to-day upkeep can't get to. It's usually worth booking one when:

  • The fabric is starting to look generally dull or grey rather than having one obvious stain.
  • There's a noticeable smell that airing the room doesn't shift.
  • You can't remember the last time it was properly cleaned, rather than just vacuumed.
  • You're preparing for visitors, a house move, or want it done before winter when windows stay shut more often.
  • A spill or stain has already set in and a quick clean-up at home hasn't fully lifted it.

As a rough guide, once or twice a year is common for upholstery that sees regular daily use, with lighter use rooms needing it less often — but the factors above matter more than any fixed interval.

FAQ

Common questions

Is vacuuming enough, or do I need a professional clean too?

Vacuuming removes surface dust and loose debris, but it doesn't reach dirt and oils worked into the fibres or fill. Regular vacuuming and a professional clean do different jobs — one is upkeep, the other is a deeper reset.

Does a light-coloured sofa need cleaning more often than a dark one?

Not necessarily more often in terms of actual soiling, but marks and general greying show up sooner on light fabric, so it often gets cleaned more often simply because it looks like it needs it sooner.

Can I just clean the sofa myself?

Regular vacuuming, cushion rotation and quick spill response are all things worth doing yourself. For a deeper clean, professional hot water extraction reaches further into the fabric than a home upholstery attachment, without over-wetting it.
