# Keyword & Search-Intent Research — carpetcleaningservice.ie (Dublin)

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Scope:** Service-area carpet & upholstery cleaning lead-gen site, Dublin, Ireland. No walk-in location.

## Methodology & honesty note

No paid keyword-volume tool (Ahrefs/SEMrush/Google Keyword Planner with real numbers) was used — none is available in this environment. All demand figures below are **reasoned estimates** from:

- Live Google.ie SERPs pulled during this research (queries run with `gl=ie&hl=en`) — local pack density, number/age of competing businesses, "People also ask," and "People also search for" are real, observed signals, not estimates.
- Known Ireland/Dublin market size (~1.5m population in Dublin city+county, ~600k households), general home-services search behaviour, and keyword-pattern heuristics (head term vs modified long-tail).
- General knowledge of Dublin geography, affluence, housing stock and rental density (source of the location tiering in Section 2) — **this is reasoned judgement, not census data**, and should be sanity-checked against CSO small-area population data before large budget is committed to it.

Anywhere a number or tier looks precise, treat it as **relative/ordinal**, not a verified figure. Where it matters, I've flagged it explicitly.

### Competitive landscape reality check (from live SERPs)

This niche is **more competitive than a generic "new local service site" assumption**. Live searches for `carpet cleaning dublin`, `upholstery cleaning dublin`, `carpet cleaning rathmines`, and `carpet cleaning donnycarney` all returned:

- A populated Google local pack (3+ businesses with 60–800+ reviews: Ace Carpet Cleaners, Happy Clean, Renew, Aqua-Dry, Chem-Dry variants, Eco Clean Solutions).
- Multiple competitors that **already run a dedicated page per Dublin neighbourhood**, including small/low-profile ones — `carpetcleaningdublin.ie`, `dublin-carpetcleaning.ie`, `dublin-housecleaning.ie`, `carpetsclean.ie`, and `CleanBee.ie` all had indexed pages for **Donnycarney**, a genuinely minor area. Some competitors (Happy Clean, Aqua-Dry, Eco Clean Solutions) instead target **Dublin postal districts** (D6, D15, D16, D18) rather than neighbourhood names.
- Real observed pricing on competitor sites: minimum call-out fees of €50–60, per-room pricing €30–65, whole-house averages quoted at €99–159 (homeconcierge.ie) and €30–60/room (ChemDry Executive). Use this range as a grounded basis for the pricing guide — it is scraped from live competitor pages, not invented.

**Implication:** thin, templated "[Area] carpet cleaning" pages with only the area name swapped will not differentiate against incumbents who already blanket the market this way. Winning requires genuine per-area specificity (streets/landmarks, housing-stock notes, real testimonials) and a stronger informational/topical layer than competitors have (most of their guide content is thin).

This is reasoned judgement from general knowledge of Dublin, not census data — validate with GSC/CSO data once the site has traffic.

## 3. Service × Location combinations worth a standalone page

Being conservative, as instructed: the default for every Tier 1/2 location page is that **all services live as sections on one page** (carpet, upholstery, mattress, rug, stain/pet/odour, end-of-tenancy), each with its own H2/anchor and a short paragraph, not a separate URL. This matches how most competitors actually structure area pages (`carpetcleaningdublin.ie`, `dublin-housecleaning.ie` bundle all services per area) and avoids diluting authority across dozens of near-duplicate thin pages.

Standalone service+location pages are only justified where the **search behaviour is genuinely different**, not just a different service word bolted on. Based on that test:

**Justified as standalone pages (launch-worthy):**
- End-of-tenancy carpet cleaning + Rathmines
- End-of-tenancy carpet cleaning + Portobello
- End-of-tenancy carpet cleaning + Ranelagh
- End-of-tenancy carpet cleaning + Drumcondra
- End-of-tenancy carpet cleaning + Phibsborough

Rationale: these five are the highest-density student/young-professional rental markets in the list, "end of tenancy" is searched by a different persona (letting agent/landlord/outgoing tenant against a lease deadline) with different urgency and proof-point needs (deposit-back guarantee, fast turnaround, invoice for agent) than a homeowner searching "carpet cleaning Rathmines" — different enough to earn a separate, differently-written page rather than just a section. Keep this list short at launch; expand only where GSC shows the section-level anchor already getting impressions for "end of tenancy" + that suburb.

**Everything else — deliberately NOT recommended as standalone pages:**
- Upholstery/sofa cleaning + any single location (no evidence of independent local search volume at neighbourhood level; keep as a section, put the standalone-page effort into the Dublin-wide upholstery pillar instead).
- Mattress cleaning + location, rug cleaning + location, pet-stain/odour + location — same reasoning; these are dublin-wide-pillar-plus-section, not location-page-plus-standalone.
- Commercial carpet cleaning + residential neighbourhood name — commercial/office buyers don't search by residential neighbourhood name; if a genuine business-district page is wanted later, target it by postal district or business park name, not the 58-area list.

## 4. Informational/guide topic shortlist (12–18)

Each rejected-for-padding candidate (e.g. "history of carpet cleaning," "types of carpet fibres A–Z") is deliberately excluded — these don't map to real searcher intent for a lead-gen site and were cut.

1. **How much does carpet cleaning cost in Dublin?** — commercial-investigative; pre-purchase price research, directly confirmed by PAA. Use real observed competitor pricing (€30–65/room, €50–60 min call-out) as the grounded baseline.
2. **How often should you get your carpets professionally cleaned?** — informational, top-of-funnel; builds recurring-customer mindset.
3. **Steam cleaning vs dry carpet cleaning: which is better?** — informational-comparative; also lets the site explain hot-water-extraction as its method of choice.
4. **How to remove red wine stains from carpet** — informational, high-frequency "emergency" search; funnels to stain-removal service page.
5. **How to remove pet urine stains and smell from carpet** — informational, emergency + emotional; funnels to pet-stain service page.
6. **How long does carpet take to dry after professional cleaning?** — informational, pre/post-purchase reassurance query; addresses a real objection (can't use the room).
7. **Can you walk on carpet right after cleaning? / aftercare tips** — informational, post-purchase; low competition, easy to answer authoritatively.
8. **End of tenancy carpet cleaning: what Dublin landlords and letting agents actually require** — informational-commercial hybrid; unique angle vs generic competitor content, ties directly to the end-of-tenancy pillar page and location spin-offs.
9. **How often should you clean your sofa/upholstery?** — informational; feeds the upholstery pillar page.
10. **How often should a mattress be cleaned, and why it matters (dust mites/allergies)** — informational; feeds mattress page, appeals to health-conscious/family searchers.
11. **Professional carpet cleaning vs. DIY rental machines (Rug Doctor etc.) — is it worth it?** — informational-comparative; directly counters the DIY alternative, a real purchase-decision fork.
12. **How to remove mould/damp smell from carpet** — informational; genuinely relevant to Dublin's climate and older housing stock, low competition, distinct from pet odour.
13. **Best way to remove chewing gum from carpet** — informational, high-frequency niche "how to" query; quick win, low competition.
14. **Coffee and tea stain removal from carpet** — informational, same pattern as wine stain; can be combined into one "common stains" hub with sub-anchors rather than many thin pages.
15. **Preparing your home or office for a professional carpet clean (checklist)** — informational-transactional bridge; reduces pre-appointment friction, good for conversion.
16. **Carpet cleaning for landlords: reducing deposit disputes in Dublin rentals** — informational-commercial hybrid aimed at the landlord persona specifically (distinct from the tenant persona in #8).
17. **Signs your carpet needs replacing vs. just needs cleaning** — informational; builds trust (not just upselling), captures a real pre-purchase question.
18. **Are professional carpet cleaning products safe for kids and pets?** — informational; addresses a real objection for the family/pet-owner segment, supports eco/child-safe positioning if genuinely true.

Recommend combining #4, #13, #14 (and other single-stain queries) into one well-structured "common carpet stains" pillar with per-stain sections/anchors rather than a fully separate URL per stain — keeps authority concentrated and matches how people actually search ("carpet stain removal guide" more than isolated single-stain head terms at this volume).

## 5. Keyword cannibalization risks

1. **Location page vs. end-of-tenancy location spin-off (same area).** E.g. "carpet cleaning Rathmines" (general area page) and "end of tenancy carpet cleaning Rathmines" (Section 3 spin-off) target overlapping queries. Mitigate: keep the spin-off narrowly focused on the tenancy/landlord persona and language, cross-link once from the general area page's end-of-tenancy section to the dedicated page (not vice-versa duplication), and give each a distinct title/meta and unique intro paragraph.

2. **Dublin-wide service pillar vs. every location page's service section.** With 44 location pages each containing an upholstery/mattress/rug/stain section, there's real risk of templated, near-duplicate boilerplate across pages competing with each other and with the Dublin-wide pillar pages for the same head terms. Mitigate: each location page's service section must contain genuinely unique area-specific content (streets, landmarks, housing type, a real local testimonial/case mention) — not just the city-wide copy with the area name swapped. The Dublin-wide pillar page should be the only page that owns the broad "[service] Dublin" head term; location pages should target "[service] [area]" long-tail only.

3. **Tier 3 areas mentioned on both their parent page and the "Areas We Cover" hub.** If both pages contain full paragraphs about, say, Dollymount, they compete with each other for that exact-match term. Mitigate: the hub page gets only a one-line mention + link per Tier 3 area; the *parent* Tier 1/2 page (e.g. Clontarf) is the only page allowed a substantive paragraph on that sub-area.

4. **"Carpet cleaning cost Dublin" guide vs. pricing mentions on every location/service page.** If every page repeats a full price table, the guide loses its ability to rank as the definitive pricing resource, and pages dilute each other. Mitigate: the guide owns the full price breakdown; all other pages link to it and show only a one-line price anchor ("from €X, see full price guide") plus a quote-request CTA.

5. **"Steam cleaning" / "hot water extraction" terminology vs. the steam-vs-dry-cleaning guide.** If every service page also tries to rank informationally for "steam cleaning," it competes with the dedicated comparison guide. Mitigate: service pages use the terms for trust/technical credibility only (1–2 sentences), the guide owns the full informational comparison.

6. **Neighbourhood-name targeting vs. a possible future move to Dublin-postal-district targeting.** Several established competitors (Happy Clean, Aqua-Dry, Eco Clean Solutions) target Dublin postal districts (D6, D15, D16, D18) instead of, or in addition to, neighbourhood names — and postal districts geographically overlap the 58-area list almost 1:1 (e.g. Rathmines ≈ D6). If this site ever adds postal-code pages later, they would directly cannibalize the neighbourhood pages. Recommendation: commit to neighbourhood-name targeting as the primary scheme (as briefed), and only ever use postal codes as secondary on-page mentions and in `areaServed`/LocalBusiness schema — never as separate landing pages.

7. **"Sofa cleaning" vs. "upholstery cleaning" as separate pages.** These are the same buyer intent in different vocabulary (confirmed by both terms appearing in each other's "People also search for"). Building two separate pages for these would cannibalize rather than expand reach. Mitigate: one page, targeting both terms naturally in copy/headings, not two pages.
