Carpet Cleaning for Landlords: Reducing Deposit Disputes in Dublin Rentals

This one's written for landlords and letting agents managing the turnaround process itself — not what a tenant should expect, but what you can do on your side, before and after a tenancy, to keep carpet condition straightforward and avoid it turning into a dispute.

Most disputes come from ambiguity, not disagreement

In our experience, the carpet-related friction at the end of a tenancy usually isn't about a genuine disagreement over what happened — it's about neither side having a clear record of what the carpet looked like before. Without something to compare against, "was this here before?" becomes a matter of opinion rather than fact, and opinions are what create disputes. Reducing ambiguity at both ends of the tenancy is the single most useful thing you can do.

Document condition at move-in and move-out

Photos of the carpet, room by room, at the start and end of every tenancy, cost you almost nothing and remove most of the guesswork later. It doesn't need to be formal — clear, dated photos taken on a phone are enough, kept alongside the rest of your inventory records. If a carpet clean is carried out at move-in or move-out, keeping confirmation of that work alongside the photos gives you a fuller, more defensible record either way.

Schedule professional cleaning between tenancies

Getting carpets professionally cleaned as a standard part of your turnaround — rather than only when a carpet looks obviously bad — does two things. It resets the baseline for the next tenancy, so any new marks are easier to identify as having happened during that tenancy rather than being inherited from before. And it means you're not relying on an outgoing tenant's own arrangements (or lack of them) to get the property back to a lettable standard.

If you manage several units, building this into a routine — the same step, every changeover — is simpler to stay on top of than deciding case by case, and gives you consistent records across your whole portfolio. See our commercial carpet cleaning page if you're coordinating this across multiple properties.

Keep the standard the same for everyone

Applying the same expectations and the same process to every tenancy — the same inspection approach, the same threshold for what counts as reasonable wear versus damage — is both fairer and easier to defend if a disagreement does come up. Being consistent matters more than being strict; a reasonable, evenly applied standard tends to avoid disputes far better than a tough one applied unevenly.

Be upfront about what a clean can and can't fix

Professional cleaning lifts dirt, everyday marks and most stains, but it doesn't reverse genuine damage — burns, worn-through patches, or staining that's set in permanently. Knowing that distinction before an inspection helps you separate "needs cleaning" from "needs replacing" quickly, rather than treating every mark the same way.

When something is genuinely in dispute

If a disagreement over carpet condition does happen, we're not the ones to advise on the legal process — that's a matter for the tenancy agreement and, where needed, Ireland's Residential Tenancies Board (RTB), which handles disputes between tenants and landlords. What good documentation and a consistent process do is reduce how often you end up needing that step in the first place.
