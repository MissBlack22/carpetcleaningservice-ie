Cleaning Methods Explained

Steam Cleaning vs Dry Carpet Cleaning: Which Is Better?

"Steam cleaning" and "dry cleaning" get compared a lot online, usually without much explanation of what either actually involves. Here's a straightforward, honest look at both — what they do, where each one is the better fit, and why hot water extraction is our default method for a proper deep clean.

What "steam cleaning" actually is

Despite the name, professional "steam cleaning" isn't cleaning with steam vapour. It's hot water extraction: a machine injects hot water mixed with a cleaning solution deep into the carpet fibres under pressure, agitates it to loosen dirt, and then immediately extracts the water back out along with the dirt, dust and residue it's lifted. Some warm vapour rises off the carpet during the process, which is likely where the "steam" name stuck, but the actual cleaning work is done by the water and extraction, not vapour alone.

It's the method we use as standard, because it reaches into the pile and backing of the carpet rather than just treating the surface, and it doesn't rely on chemical residue being left behind to keep working after we've gone.

What dry and low-moisture cleaning involves

"Dry" carpet cleaning is a bit of a loose term that covers a few different low-moisture methods, most commonly:

  • Encapsulation — a cleaning solution is applied and agitated into the carpet, where it crystallises around dirt particles as it dries. The crystallised residue and the dirt it's trapped are then vacuumed away.
  • Dry compound cleaning — an absorbent cleaning compound (a fine powder or granular product) is worked into the carpet with a machine, absorbs dirt and moisture, and is then vacuumed out.

Both use little to no water compared with hot water extraction, which is the main appeal: carpet stays close to dry throughout, and rooms can usually go back into use almost immediately.

Comparing the two honestly

Drying time

Dry and low-moisture methods win here — carpet is typically dry within an hour or two. Hot water extraction leaves carpet damp, usually needing several hours (sometimes longer with poor ventilation) to dry fully.

Depth of clean

Hot water extraction generally reaches deeper into the pile and backing, making it the stronger choice for a genuine deep clean, heavy soiling, or carpet that hasn't been professionally cleaned in a long time.

Delicate fibres

Some natural fibres and specific rug or carpet types are more sensitive to moisture and heat. A low-moisture method, applied carefully, can be the safer option — we'll assess the fibre before recommending either approach.

Time-sensitive spaces

Offices, shops and other spaces that can't stay closed for long benefit from the faster turnaround of low-moisture cleaning, even if it means a lighter clean than hot water extraction would give.

Residue

Because hot water extraction rinses and extracts, it leaves comparatively little behind. Encapsulation and compound methods rely on a product remaining in the carpet briefly before being vacuumed out.

Frequency

Low-moisture methods suit more frequent, lighter maintenance cleaning between deeper cleans, particularly in busy commercial spaces with high foot traffic.

Which one should you choose?

For most homes, and for a genuine deep clean anywhere, hot water extraction is the better all-round choice — it's why it's our standard method for residential carpet cleaning. For offices and commercial spaces that need to reopen quickly, or as a lighter maintenance clean between full cleans, a low-moisture approach can make more practical sense. Neither method is universally "better" — it depends on the carpet, the level of soiling, and how much time you have before the room needs to be back in use.

If you're not sure which suits your situation, tell us about the space and the carpet when you get in touch — we'll recommend an approach rather than default to one method regardless of the job.

Common questions

Is steam cleaning actually steam?

Not literally. What's commonly called "steam cleaning" is hot water extraction — hot water and a cleaning solution are injected into the carpet under pressure and immediately extracted back out, along with the dirt. There's warm vapour involved, but the cleaning is done by the water and extraction, not steam vapour on its own.

Which method dries faster?

Dry and low-moisture methods dry faster, typically within an hour or two, because they use little to no water. Hot water extraction leaves carpet damp and usually takes several hours to dry fully, depending on ventilation and carpet type.

Which method cleans deeper?

Hot water extraction generally reaches deeper into the carpet pile and backing, which is why it's the standard choice for a thorough deep clean. Dry methods work well for surface-level maintenance and refreshing carpet between deeper cleans.
